# Playbook: Narrated Demo

A confident voice walks the viewer through the product while the screen shows it working.

## When to use
- Demo requests, trial signups, or feature awareness.
- When the ad needs to make a verbal argument, for example "the system handles the request before anyone opens a ticket."
- When there are several capabilities to show, or one capability to go deep on.
- Sound-on placements such as in-stream video.

## Core DNA (always true)
1. Script first, storyboard second. Every visual beat is locked to a voiceover phrase.
2. The voice is the spine. Remove it and the ad loses its argument.
3. Scene cuts land on sentence boundaries. One clause per scene.
4. Visuals show, the voice tells. No on-screen text that repeats what is being said.
5. Music sits under the voice at about 18 percent. Three layers, one job each: voice makes the claim, visuals prove it, music sets the mood.
6. One punchline animation, saved for the moment the voice says the key word.
7. The closer is still.

## Default patterns
Hook A1 or A4. Structure B1 for 30 seconds, B4 for 15 seconds, B1 or B3 for 59 seconds. Color C1, C2, or C3. Composition D1 with screenshots, D4 plus D5 plus D8 without. Motion E1, E2, E4, E9, E10. Voice G1, G4, G5, G6, G7, G9, G10. Action H2 with H3 or H4. Trust I1 and one of I2, I4, I5.

## Voice craft
- 2.5 to 2.7 words per second.
- Bridge sentences across cuts.
- Use single-word pivots to vary rhythm.
- Never speak the web address.
- Music fades 1.2 seconds before the end.

## Done when
- The sync map reads one row per scene, one phrase per scene.
- The brand name and the core promise both land before second five.
- One real animation lands on the key word.
- The closer holds still for at least two seconds.
